measured opening without useful CPU fragment specialization. This candidate

<!-- CodexAstraLocal: Physical ownership, cumulative compiler work and shader-module limits are distinct bounds; retirement cannot refund work or expose a ninth live owner. -->

The bank owns at most eight CPU pipelines, including building, failed, retired
and destruction-queued entries. A fixed 64-entry observation table requires
16 requests across four recent swap intervals before admission. New admissions
are spaced by at least four intervals. Under actual capacity pressure, one
unused entry can retire after eight intervals of inactivity; terminal failed
entries are preferred. Exact equality checks protect against hash collisions.
Lifetime work is limited to 64 CPU attempts and 256 combined CPU/GPU attempts,
with failures charged and no refunds. The shared fragment-module limit stays 128.
Ready hits remain usable when admission limits are reached.

<!-- CodexAstraLocal: Describe each independent lifetime condition and why post-draw stamping is required when stream mapping can submit earlier commands. -->

Retirement runs immediately after the existing command-worker drain. After the
compiler's final owner release, it removes lookup and cached binding references
at that drained boundary. Completed GPU use separately permits queued destruction.
Binding and actual draw enqueue both record use, covering an intervening stream-buffer flush.
Destruction uses the existing optional worker and retains the slot until worker
completion. No new GPU wait, command flush or synchronous compile is introduced.
Generation tokens reject stale references; failed queue admission retains safe
ownership and the complete fallback.
